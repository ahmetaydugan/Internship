## Day 2 - Attention Is All You Need:
On the second day of the week, I dove into one of the most critical milestones in modern AI history. Today was entirely focused on understanding the architecture that powers today's Large Language Models (LLMs) and how machines learned to process context simultaneously rather than sequentially.

1. Historical Concept: The 2017 Paper
I studied the legendary 2017 paper, "Attention Is All You Need," which introduced the Transformer architecture. On a conceptual level, I learned what the attention mechanism is and how a model distributes its "focus" across other words in a sentence while processing a specific word. I also understood why this breakthrough successfully overcame the sequential processing limitation of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), where the model had to finish processing one word before moving on to the next.

2. Academic Block: The Illustrated Transformer
To visualize these complex theoretical concepts, I read Jay Alammar's "The Illustrated Transformer." This piece beautifully broke down the internal workings of the architecture, making the abstract mathematics behind self-attention mechanisms much easier to digest and comprehend.

3. Practical Visualization: Attention Weights
Instead of building a Transformer from scratch, my practical task today was to visually understand the mechanism in action. I examined an attention-weight visualization using a sample heatmap. Seeing exactly how much "attention" one specific word pays to every other word in a given sentence provided a crystal-clear understanding of how modern NLP models capture deep contextual relationships.
